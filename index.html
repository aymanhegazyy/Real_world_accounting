GENERAL LEDGER & CLOSING DESK

Your day as GL Accountant

Make sure sub-ledgers and journals become complete, correctly classified statements. Investigate, adjust and close — never hide differences.

Focus: journals · control accounts · close Control: every balance needs an owner + evidence

Daily / close checklistStart here

  • Review posted journals and rejected interfaces.
  • Check suspense, unusual balances and manual entries.
  • Close AR, AP, inventory, payroll and treasury sub-ledgers.
  • Post accruals, prepayments, depreciation and reclassifications.
1 · Sub-ledgers 2 · Adjustments 3 · Reconcile 4 · Trial balance 5 · Statements

On your desk todayOpen cases

Accrual review — unbilled service

Service received in the period; invoice not yet arrived. Assess materiality and support.

Control account vs sub-ledger difference

Investigate timing, missing journals or coding errors before close.

Company setup & structure

Fiscal year, functional currency, departments and bank structure underpin every close.

Core actions on this desk

Key control: Every balance must have an owner, a reconciliation, supporting evidence and an explanation for movement. Differences are investigated, not forced to zero.

Quick decision cards — GL

Accrue or not?

Service in period, invoice next month.

→ If material + reliable estimate → accrue; reverse later.
Prepaid vs expense

Annual insurance paid in full today.

→ Prepaid asset; amortize monthly to expense.
Force-matching a control gap

Sub-ledger and GL differ at close.

→ Never plug. Build the bridge; assign owners.
Suspense still open

Material balance in suspense at period-end.

→ Age lines; clear with evidence before signing close.
ACCOUNTS RECEIVABLE DESK

Your day as AR Accountant

Turn sales and bank receipts into clean, collectible customer balances. Start with evidence, never guess the allocation.

Focus: invoices · receipts · aging Control: no self-approval of credit / write-offs

Morning checklistStart here

  • Open AR aging and list overdue invoices by customer.
  • Review unapplied / unallocated bank receipts.
  • Check credit holds and customers near their limit.
  • Confirm delivery evidence waiting for invoice.
1 · Evidence 2 · Invoice / Receipt 3 · Allocate 4 · Reconcile 5 · Follow up

On your desk todayOpen cases

Unallocated receipt — EGP 85,000

Bank reference only. Need remittance advice before allocation.

Customer says “we already paid”

Invoice still open. Trace receipt, allocation and statement.

New customer request pending approval

Credit limit and documents must be validated before activation.

Core actions on this desk

Key control: Never allocate unidentified cash by assumption. Park in suspense, obtain remittance advice, then match to open invoices.

Accounting impact — Accounts Receivable

How AR hits the Chart of Accounts & financial statements
Chart of Accounts
  • 120000 Accounts Receivable BS Asset
  • 410000 Sales Revenue IS
  • 110000 Cash & Banks BS Asset
  • Suspense / Unapplied receipts (when allocation unknown)
Balance Sheet
  • ↑ AR asset when you invoice on credit
  • ↓ AR and ↑ Bank when customer pays
  • Credit limits protect overstated collectible assets
Income Statement
  • ↑ Sales revenue on invoice (with output VAT if applicable)
  • Bad-debt / ECL expense if collectability deteriorates
  • Cash receipt itself is not revenue — only clears the asset
Typical entries
Dr 120000 Accounts Receivable  Cr 410000 Sales Revenue
Dr 110000 Bank  Cr 120000 Accounts Receivable
Unknown receipt: Dr Bank  Cr Unapplied Receipts (Suspense)

Quick decision cards — AR

Unidentified bank receipt

Only bank reference — no customer remittance yet.

→ Park in unapplied / suspense. Never credit revenue.
Customer says “already paid”

Invoice still open on aging.

→ Get remittance + search receipts by amount/date across customers.
GL AR ≠ aging

Control account does not equal open invoices.

→ Open AR bridge. Explain unapplied, credits, manual GL, FX.
Short payment

Cash received less than invoice with no debit note.

→ Allocate receipt; leave balance open; request deduction proof.
ACCOUNTS PAYABLE DESK

Your day as AP Accountant

Protect the company from duplicate, unsupported or early payments. Three-way match before you post.

Focus: PO · GRN · invoice · payment Control: no payment without independent approval

Morning checklistStart here

  • Review invoices received and blocked invoices.
  • Check unmatched GRNs and goods received not invoiced.
  • Scan due dates, cash priorities and supplier statements.
  • Flag duplicate invoice numbers or bank details.
1 · PO 2 · GRN 3 · Invoice match 4 · Post / hold 5 · Payment proposal

On your desk todayOpen cases

Invoice without GRN

Three-way match incomplete. Do not post until warehouse confirms receipt.

Supplier statement difference

Reconcile open items before releasing the payment proposal.

New supplier master request

Tax card, commercial registration and bank details required.

Core actions on this desk

Key control: PO → GRN / service confirmation → Invoice. Incomplete match = hold. Payment preparation and payment approval stay separated.

Accounting impact — Accounts Payable

Liabilities, expenses / inventory and payment effects
Chart of Accounts
  • 210000 Accounts Payable BS Liability
  • 130000 Inventory BS Asset
  • 530000 Operating Expenses IS
  • 230000 Accrued Expenses BS Liability
  • 110000 Cash & Banks BS Asset
Balance Sheet
  • ↑ AP liability when invoice is accepted
  • ↑ Inventory (or asset) on goods received
  • ↓ AP and ↓ Bank on payment
  • GRNI / accruals when goods received but not invoiced
Income Statement
  • Expense hits P&L when service/period cost is recognized
  • Inventory purchases do not hit expense until sold (COGS)
  • Early or duplicate payment does not create expense — it misstates cash and AP
Typical entries
Dr 130000 Inventory / 530000 Expense  Cr 210000 Accounts Payable
Payment: Dr 210000 Accounts Payable  Cr 110000 Bank

Quick decision cards — AP

Invoice without GRN

Three-way match incomplete.

→ Hold. Request GRN or reject. Do not pay on pressure.
GRNI at month-end

Goods in warehouse, supplier invoice not in.

→ Accrue inventory/expense and liability; reverse on real invoice.
Duplicate invoice risk

Same supplier + number or same amount/date.

→ Block; match payment proposal to open items only.
Price variance vs PO

Invoice price higher than approved PO.

→ Route to procurement; post matched amount only.
INVENTORY & WAREHOUSE DESK

Your day as Inventory Accountant

Keep a reliable quantity and value trail from receipt to issue. No adjustment without documented approval.

Focus: GRN · issues · counts · COGS Control: quantity first, then value

Morning checklistStart here

  • Review open POs and expected receipts.
  • Check low-stock, blocked, damaged and negative balances.
  • Confirm pending material requests and issue notes.
  • Prepare cycle-count locations for the day.
1 · Receive 2 · Put away 3 · Issue 4 · Count 5 · Value / COGS

On your desk todayOpen cases

GRN pending for supplier invoice

Warehouse must confirm quantity and condition before AP posts.

Count variance on high-value item

Investigate location, batch and last movements before any write-off.

New item master request

Category, UOM and standard cost needed before purchasing.

Core actions on this desk

Key control: Physical quantity evidence drives accounting. Adjustments and write-offs need investigation and independent approval.

Accounting impact — Inventory & Warehouse

Quantity drives value; value drives COGS and assets
Chart of Accounts
  • 130000 Inventory BS Asset
  • 510000 Cost of Goods Sold IS
  • 210000 Accounts Payable (via purchases) BS
  • Inventory write-down / variance expense when approved
Balance Sheet
  • ↑ Inventory on GRN / receipt at cost
  • ↓ Inventory on issue / sale / approved write-off
  • Count variances adjust the asset after investigation
Income Statement
  • ↑ COGS when inventory is issued to cost of sales
  • Write-downs and approved losses hit expense
  • Wrong quantity = wrong COGS and wrong profit
Typical entries
Receipt: Dr 130000 Inventory  Cr 210000 AP / GRNI
Issue to COGS: Dr 510000 Cost of Goods Sold  Cr 130000 Inventory

Quick decision cards — Inventory

Count ≠ system

Physical qty differs from books.

→ Investigate movements first; then adjust with approval.
Negative stock

Issue posted without receipt.

→ Block process; post missing GRN or reverse issue.
GL inventory ≠ valuation

Control account off vs stock report.

→ Reconcile qty×cost; check unposted receipts and cost variances.
Cut-off on GRN

Goods in warehouse, GRN next period.

→ Accrue in correct period before close.
TREASURY & BANKS DESK

Your day as Treasury Accountant

Protect cash, explain every bank movement, and keep preparation, approval, execution and reconciliation separated.

Focus: receipts · payments · position · recon Control: maker-checker on every payment batch

Morning checklistStart here

  • Confirm opening balances on all bank accounts (EGP / USD / EUR / GBP).
  • Update daily cash position and urgent payment commitments.
  • Review overdraft limits, returned items and bank alerts.
  • List unidentified receipts that need AR allocation evidence.
1 · Position 2 · Receipts 3 · Payments 4 · Post fees / FX 5 · Reconcile

On your desk todayOpen cases

Unknown bank receipt EGP 85,000

Cash is in the bank. AR must identify customer and invoice before full allocation.

Payment batch awaiting dual approval

Verify beneficiary details and signatory limits before release.

New bank account request

Classify nature first: current account = Asset; loan/overdraft = Liability.

Core actions on this desk

Key control: Every bank line must have an owner and a story. Unidentified items stay in suspense until evidence arrives — never force them into revenue or expense.

Accounting impact — Treasury & Banks

Cash is an asset; every movement must map to a counterparty account
Chart of Accounts
  • 110000 Cash & Banks BS Asset
  • 220000 Bank Loans & Overdrafts BS Liability
  • 120000 / 210000 AR / AP (clearing) BS
  • Bank charges / interest expense or income
Balance Sheet
  • ↑ Bank on customer/other receipts
  • ↓ Bank on supplier, payroll or expense payments
  • Loan/overdraft facilities sit under liabilities with a limit
  • Unidentified items stay in suspense — not forced into P&L
Income Statement
  • Bank fees → expense; interest on loans → finance cost
  • FX differences on foreign currency accounts may hit P&L
  • A receipt is not revenue until the nature is proven
Typical entries
Receipt: Dr 110000 Bank  Cr 120000 AR (or other source)
Payment: Dr 210000 AP / Expense / Payroll payable  Cr 110000 Bank
Fee: Dr Bank charges expense  Cr 110000 Bank

Quick decision cards — Treasury

Book ≠ bank statement

Classic reconciling difference.

→ Open bank bridge. Post fees; age outstanding items.
Unknown deposit

Money in bank, no customer known.

→ Suspense → ticket to AR with bank reference.
Wrong beneficiary paid

Payment left the account to wrong IBAN.

→ Recall with bank now; tighten maker-checker on master data.
Stale outstanding cheque

Open on recon for many months.

→ Contact payee; reverse or reissue per policy.
PAYROLL & HR DESK

Your day as Payroll Accountant

Convert approved attendance, salary and deductions into a controlled payroll run. Sensitive changes need independent approval.

Focus: master data · hours · deductions · GL Control: freeze inputs before calculate

Morning checklistStart here

  • Review new hires, leavers and approved pay changes.
  • Validate attendance, shifts, overtime and leave approvals.
  • Check department / cost center mapping for each employee.
  • Send exceptions to HR / Finance approver before run.
1 · Master data 2 · Attendance 3 · Calculate 4 · Approve 5 · Post & pay

On your desk todayOpen cases

Hours don't match (160 vs 172)

Approved attendance differs from payroll input. Validate overtime or correct the error.

New employee onboarding

ID, social insurance, department, pay basis and hire date required.

Department cost center confirmation

Wrong cost center distorts departmental expense and management reports.

Core actions on this desk

Key control: Source approvals (attendance, overtime, changes) must agree with the payroll register and the GL liability before payment is released.

Accounting impact — Payroll & HR

Expense and statutory liabilities before cash leaves the bank
Chart of Accounts
  • 520000 Salaries & Wages IS Expense
  • Payroll payable BS Liability
  • 240000 Taxes & Social Insurance Payable BS Liability
  • 110000 Bank (on net pay) BS Asset
Balance Sheet
  • ↑ Payroll / tax / SI liabilities when payroll is recognized
  • ↓ Those liabilities and ↓ Bank on payment
  • Wrong cost center misstates departmental balances, not total equity alone
Income Statement
  • ↑ Salaries & wages expense for the period earned
  • Overtime / shifts increase expense only if approved and supported
  • Paying the bank does not create extra expense — it settles the liability
Typical entry
Dr 520000 Salaries & Wages  Cr Payroll Payable / 240000 Tax & SI Payable
Payment: Dr Payroll Payable  Cr 110000 Bank

Quick decision cards — Payroll

Hours mismatch

Approved attendance ≠ payroll file.

→ Stop run; correct with HR approval; recalculate.
Wrong cost center

Department P&L looks odd; company total OK.

→ Fix employee master mapping; reclass if needed.
Liability not clearing

After paying payroll, payable remains.

→ Match bank payment coding to liability accounts.
Late joiner / leaver

HR change after payroll freeze.

→ Off-cycle adjustment with documented approval.

Live balances

Daily Cash Box — Cash Collection

Any cash coming into the till outside a formal AR receipt (walk-in cash sales, other income, returned advances).

01
CollectionThe cashier collects the cash and issues a numbered receipt voucher on the spot.
02
Supporting DocumentThe receipt voucher number and the source of the cash must be recorded before posting.
03
Countersign & PostA supervisor other than the cashier countersigns the voucher, then it is posted.
Red flags
  • Missing or unnumbered receipt voucher.
  • Same person collects the cash and countersigns the voucher (no segregation of duties).

Daily Cash Box — Cash Expense

Small cash disbursements paid straight out of the till.

01
RequestThe requester submits the expense with its purpose before any cash leaves the till.
02
Supporting DocumentA receipt or invoice number is mandatory — no document, no payment.
03
Approval & PaymentApproved within the petty-cash authority, then paid and posted.
Red flags
  • No supporting receipt or invoice number.
  • Amount exceeds the petty-cash authorization limit (demo limit: 1,000 EGP) — needs Finance Manager approval instead.
  • Splitting one expense into several smaller ones to stay under the limit.

Treasury & Banks

Treasury Accountant — Operating Cycle

A practical cash-and-bank control cycle from opening position to month-end reconciliation.

Daily opening and liquidity review

  1. Review opening balances: bank statements, cash-on-hand, petty cash and approved overdraft limits.
  2. Update the cash position: expected collections, approved payments, payroll, taxes and urgent commitments.
  3. Review bank alerts: returned cheques, charges, direct debits, interest and unidentified credits.
  4. Confirm authority: payment batches and transfers are supported by approved requests and signatory limits.

Daily receipts, payments and transfers

  1. Record cash and bank receipts against customer remittances or other approved sources.
  2. Prepare supplier, payroll, tax and expense payments from approved payment proposals.
  3. Execute bank transfers only after maker-checker approval and beneficiary verification.
  4. Record bank fees, interest, exchange differences and returned payments with evidence.
  5. Maintain a controlled cheque register and daily cash count where applicable.
Daily output: cash position, payment register, receipt register, transfer log and exceptions list.

Daily bank reconciliation

  • Compare bank portal/statement activity to the cashbook and posted journal entries.
  • Identify deposits in transit, outstanding cheques, bank charges and timing differences.
  • Investigate unidentified items; do not force-match or alter dates to clear a difference.
  • Escalate unusual transactions, duplicate payments and unauthorized changes immediately.
Key control: the person preparing a payment must not be the sole approver or bank reconciler.

Month-end treasury close

  1. Obtain bank confirmations/statements for every active account, including zero-balance accounts.
  2. Complete bank reconciliations and age all outstanding reconciling items.
  3. Reconcile cashbook and bank sub-ledgers to the general ledger.
  4. Accrue bank charges and interest; record foreign currency revaluation where applicable.
  5. Confirm loans/overdraft balances, interest, covenant data and facility limits.
  6. Prepare cash-flow inputs and a month-end liquidity forecast for Finance Management.

Control documents and evidence

  • Approved payment request ↔ payment batch ↔ bank confirmation.
  • Customer remittance ↔ bank credit ↔ AR receipt allocation.
  • Bank statement ↔ bank reconciliation ↔ cashbook/GL.
  • Loan agreement ↔ lender statement ↔ interest schedule.
Segregation of duties: Treasury prepares and executes within authority; Finance approves; Accounting posts/reviews; bank access is role-based and periodically reviewed.

Weekly management view

  • 13-week cash forecast and minimum cash buffer.
  • Expected collections versus committed payments.
  • Upcoming payroll, tax, loan and supplier maturities.
  • Available facilities, overdraft utilization and concentration by bank.
Management output: liquidity dashboard, funding gaps, surplus cash actions and overdue reconciliation items.

Practical cases & problems — AR desk

Problems the AR accountant meets on a normal day — with why they happen, how to investigate, and the correct treatment.

1GL Accounts Receivable ≠ Invoice aging total
Why it happens

Aging is built from open invoices. The GL control also holds unapplied receipts, unallocated credit notes, GL-only journals, FX revaluation and timing differences.

How to investigate
  1. Compare sum of customer sub-ledger balances to GL 120000.
  2. Extract unapplied cash and open credit notes — they affect GL but may not show as open invoices in aging.
  3. List manual journals to 120000 without customer lines.
  4. Check FX and period cut-off.
Solution

Bridge: Open invoices + Unapplied receipts − Unallocated credits ± Manual GL ± FX ± Timing = GL. Clear with evidence; never force zero.

2Customer says “we already paid” but invoice still open
Why it happens

Cash on wrong customer, left unapplied, allocated to another invoice, or paid under a different reference.

How to investigate
  1. Obtain bank advice + remittance (invoice numbers and amounts).
  2. Search receipts by amount, date and bank reference across customers.
  3. Review unapplied cash and credits on that customer.
Solution

Reallocate with evidence. Transfer between customers only with approval. Do not write off until the payment trail is complete.

3Unapplied / unidentified receipt
Solution

Park in unapplied/suspense. Ask sales/customer for allocation. Never post to revenue by guess.

Dr Bank    Cr Unapplied receipts
4Credit note / dispute not reflected correctly
Solution

Raise credit note against the invoice or keep both open for net allocation. Mark dispute for collection. Aging policy should net open invoices and credits.

5Customer over credit limit / order blocked
Why it happens

Open invoices + new order exceed approved limit; or limit never updated after risk review.

Solution

Do not bypass the block yourself. Collect overdue amounts, request limit increase from credit control with support, or take advance payment. Document the decision.

6Partial payment / short payment without explanation
Solution

Allocate the received amount to the invoice; leave the balance open. Ask the customer for deduction details (debit note, discount, return). Do not write off the shortfall without approval and evidence.

7FX customer balance looks wrong after rate change
Solution

Open items in foreign currency are revalued at closing rate; the difference hits FX gain/loss, not the customer’s invoice currency balance. Check revaluation run and which rate was used.

8Bad debt / expected credit loss review
Solution

Use aging, dispute status and customer risk. Raise provision with approval — do not delete the invoice. Write-off only when recovery is exhausted and authorized.

7Wrong tax / VAT on supplier invoice
Solution

Do not post incorrect VAT to input tax. Request corrected tax invoice. Temporary hold or post net only per local rules until valid document arrives.

8Payment proposal includes blocked or disputed invoices
Solution

Filter payment run to approved open items only. Keep segregation: who prepares ≠ who approves. Remove blocked lines before bank file.

Inventory

Practical cases & problems — Inventory desk

Quantity, valuation, COGS and cut-off issues.

1Physical count ≠ system quantity
Why it happens

Unposted GRN/issues, wrong bin, damage/theft, cut-off, wrong UOM.

Solution

Investigate movements first; post missing documents; then adjust with approval. Value variance per policy to expense/COGS.

2Inventory GL ≠ stock valuation report
Solution

Reconcile qty × cost to GL 130000. Check unposted receipts, standard/actual variances, and naked GL journals.

3Negative stock balance
Solution

Block negative issues. Post missing receipt or reverse wrong issue. Fix process and access rights.

4COGS looks too high or too low vs sales
Solution

Check issue postings, returns, cut-off (sale in period vs issue next period), and cost method. Wrong timing misstates margin.

5Obsolete / slow-moving / damaged stock
Solution

Identify from age and condition reports. Propose write-down or write-off with management approval and evidence. Do not leave dead stock at full cost.

6Cut-off: GRN after period-end for goods already in warehouse
Solution

Accrue receipt in the correct period (inventory + GRNI/AP). Align physical and books before close.

Treasury & Banks

Practical cases & problems — Treasury desk

Reconciliation, unidentified cash, payments and liquidity.

1Book balance ≠ Bank statement
Why it happens

Outstanding payments, deposits in transit, unposted fees/interest, direct debits, errors, duplicates.

Solution

Formal reconciliation: statement → + deposits in transit − outstanding payments → post missing bank items → age old open items.

2Unidentified bank receipt
Solution

Post to suspense/unapplied; hand to AR with reference. Do not credit revenue until proven.

3Wrong beneficiary / payment recall
Solution

Stop/recall with bank immediately. Strengthen maker-checker and supplier bank master changes.

4Stale outstanding cheque / long reconciling item
Solution

Age every open reconciling item. Contact payee/bank. Reverse or reissue per policy after approval — do not leave indefinitely.

5Bank charges / interest not posted
Solution

Pick from statement during reconciliation; post to expense/finance cost with supporting advice.

Dr Bank charges / Interest expense    Cr Bank
6Overdraft / limit breach risk
Solution

Update daily cash position before large payment runs. Prioritize payments; escalate limit or delay non-critical payments with approval.

Payroll & HR

Practical cases & problems — Payroll desk

Attendance, calculations, cost centers and statutory liabilities.

1Approved hours ≠ payroll input
Solution

Stop the run. Compare attendance/OT approvals to the file. Correct with documented approval; recalculate control totals.

2Wrong department / cost center on payroll expense
Solution

Fix employee master mapping. Reclass expense if period already posted. Company total may be fine while department P&L is wrong.

3Payroll payable / tax does not clear after payment
Solution

Match bank payments to liability accounts. Check partial pays, wrong GL coding, prior balances. Reconcile register → liability → bank.

4New joiner / leaver missed in the payroll cycle
Solution

HR cut-off list before freeze. Pro-rate joiners/leavers. Post off-cycle adjustment if already paid wrongly.

5Unauthorized salary change in the file
Solution

Sensitive changes need independent approval and audit trail. Compare current run to prior master; investigate unexplained deltas before payment.

6Social insurance / tax deduction mismatch vs schedule
Solution

Recalculate from gross and rules. Fix employee statutory data. Reconcile liability to payment and filing schedule.

General Ledger & Closing

Practical cases & problems — GL desk

Accruals, prepayments, control accounts, suspense and close.

1Accrual — expense belongs to this month, invoice later
Why it matters

Without accrual, expense is understated and liabilities missing in the period of consumption.

How to decide
  1. Evidence of service/period (contract, usage, confirmation).
  2. Materiality under policy.
  3. Reliable estimate.
Solution

Accrue at month-end; reverse next period or on invoice; true-up difference.

Dr Expense    Cr Accrued expenses (230000)
2Prepayment — paid now, benefit in future months
Solution

Record prepaid asset; amortize monthly over the benefit period (insurance, rent in advance).

Dr Prepaid    Cr Bank
Each month: Dr Expense    Cr Prepaid
3Control account ≠ sub-ledger (AR / AP / Inventory / Bank)
Solution

Never force-match with a balancing journal. Identify unposted batches, GL-only entries, FX, cut-off. Owner + due date per item.

4Suspense not cleared at month-end
Solution

Age every line. Clear to the correct account with evidence. Material suspense blocks a clean close.

5Missed depreciation or wrong asset cut-off
Solution

Run depreciation for the period; check additions/disposals dates. Post catch-up if missed.

Dr Depreciation expense    Cr Accumulated depreciation
6Revenue or expense in the wrong period (cut-off)
Solution

Test last/first invoices, GRNs and service periods around period-end. Accrue or defer so recognition matches performance/consumption.

7Reclassification — balance sheet vs P&L misposting
Solution

Example: long-term deposit posted to expense, or supplier advance to expense. Reclass with support; do not leave analytical distortions.

8Trial balance balances but financial statements still look wrong
Solution

Balanced TB only means debits = credits. Still review analytics, reconciliations, cut-off, provisions and disclosure. Wrong classification can still “balance”.
